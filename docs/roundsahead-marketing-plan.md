# RoundsAhead — Marketing & Distribution Plan

Companion to `roundsahead-launch-plan.md` (see Track B). This document covers the
counselor channel, the conference calendar, and SEO in operational detail.

**Positioning:** RoundsAhead is not a college prep app. It is *pre-health pathway
planning* — for families whose student is aiming at medicine, nursing, CRNA, PA,
or allied health. Every marketing decision below follows from that narrowness. The name says it:
*rounds* is the vocabulary of the destination, *ahead* is the promise to the parent.
Generic college prep competes with free tools and loses.

**Buyer:** the parent. **Channel:** the counselor. Those are different people
and the plan treats them differently.

---

## Part 0 — Messaging and taglines

"Rounds" is opaque to anyone outside medicine. The tagline's job is to decode
the name and name the audience — not to cheer. Three surfaces, three jobs.

### App Store subtitle (~30 character cap on iOS)

> **Pre-health college planning** (27)

Alternate: **The pre-health path, mapped** (27)

Say the category plainly. This is a search and scanning surface, not a place
for voice.

### Website hero — where the parent decides

> # The road to medicine starts in high school.

This does four things at once: decodes "rounds," names the audience, speaks to
the parent rather than the student, and makes the sales argument implicitly —
that waiting until senior year is already too late. That last part is worth
more than any adjective.

Alternate, if you want more momentum:

> **Rounds are years away. The path starts now.**

### Counselor handout — one line, no cleverness

> For students headed into medicine, nursing, and allied health.

Counselors see a lot of vendors and discount enthusiasm on sight. Plain
beats clever here.

### In-app encouragement

> **Let's get you there.**

Good copy, wrong surface for positioning. Use it where the student is the
reader and the context is already established — onboarding, or the moment a
student completes their profile or hits a milestone.

### Voice rules

- [ ] **Address the parent on buying surfaces, the student inside the app.**
      The buyer and the user are different people. Most marketing copy failures
      here come from talking to the student where the parent is reading.
- [ ] ❌ **No exclamation points in marketing copy.** Your credibility play is
      being the honest one — the product that says med-school acceptance rates
      are gamed and that loans get disguised as awards on an aid letter. Peppy
      undercuts that, and counselors notice.
- [ ] Never repeat the product name inside the tagline. It's already on screen
      and you don't have the characters.
- [ ] Every claim in marketing copy should be one you can defend with a source,
      for the same reason every pathway fact carries a `sourceUrl`.

## Part 1 — The counselor channel

### Why counselors, when parents pay

One counselor who likes the app puts it in front of 200 families at once. The
counselor never pays anything. This is a distribution channel wearing the
costume of a customer segment — don't confuse the two, and don't try to monetize
it.

### What counselors get, free and forever

- [ ] Counselor tier: multi-student roster view, no payment, no trial expiry
- [ ] **Printable per-pathway PDF handouts.** Counselors love handouts. Every
      one carries roundsahead.com into a family's kitchen. This is the cheapest, most
      underrated asset in this entire plan.
- [ ] A one-page "what is this" sheet they can forward to parents. Lead with
      the counselor line from Part 0: *For students headed into medicine,
      nursing, and allied health.*
- [ ] Nothing that requires them to administer, configure, or support anything

### Target counselor segments, in priority order

1. **HOSA chapter advisors** — the bullseye. HOSA-Future Health Professionals is
   a health-science student organization with chapters in most states, and its
   advisors are health-science teachers whose entire student population is your
   target market. Nobody else in college prep is this precisely targetable.
2. **Health science magnet programs and career academies** — same logic, direct
   outreach, often a single named coordinator per school.
3. **Independent educational consultants (IECs)** who specialize in pre-health.
   Small in number, enormous influence per person, and they talk to each other.
4. **General high school counselors** — largest group, least targeted. A
   counselor with 400 students covers scheduling, mental health, and crises;
   pre-health is a thin slice of their attention. Reach them last.

### Phase 1: ten counselors, not ten thousand

The goal of the first phase is **one counselor who actually uses it and tells
you why the others won't.** Do not build counselor features before this.

- [ ] Identify 10 HOSA advisors, ideally in your own region so you can meet some
      in person
- [ ] Personal, individual email. Name their program. Explain you built it for
      your own kid. That story is your single best asset — use it.
- [ ] Ask for 20 minutes of feedback, not adoption. Adoption asks get ignored;
      feedback asks get accepted.
- [ ] ⚠️ **Do not blast school email addresses.** District filters are
      aggressive, and a spam flag can burn the channel permanently. Personal,
      individual, low-volume.
- [ ] Ask each one: what would make you show this to a student? Then build that.

### Phase 2: chapter and state level

- [ ] State HOSA associations — most have advisor newsletters and listservs
- [ ] Offer to present a free session at a state HOSA event
- [ ] Sponsor a state HOSA competitive event (cheap, high goodwill, hard to buy
      any other way)

## Part 2 — Conference calendar

⚠️ **Name collision — check this before you register or pay anything.**
"ASCA" resolves to at least three different organizations. The school counselor
one is the **American School Counselor Association** (schoolcounselor.org,
conference site ascaconferences.org). `ascaconference.com` is the **Association
for Student Conduct Administration** — a completely unrelated group. Verify the
domain matches the org before sending money.

### The calendar

| Event | Dates | Location | Audience | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **State HOSA SLCs** | Feb–Apr, varies by state | Statewide | Health-science advisors + students | ⭐⭐⭐ **Start here** |
| **Regional ACAC conferences** | Feb–Apr (spring) | Regional | HS counselors + admissions | ⭐⭐⭐ |
| **HOSA ILC 2027** | June 22–25, 2027 | Pennsylvania Convention Center, Philadelphia, PA | ~All HOSA advisors + students | ⭐⭐⭐ |
| **ASCA Annual 2027** | July 17–20, 2027 | Greater Columbus Convention Center, Columbus, OH | School counselors, broad | ⭐⭐ |
| **NACAC 2026** | Oct 8–10, 2026 | Minneapolis Convention Center, Minneapolis, MN | Admissions-heavy | ⭐ |
| **NACAC 2027** | Sept 23–25, 2027 | Kansas City Convention Center, Kansas City, MO | Admissions-heavy | ⭐ |
| **HOSA ILC 2028** | June 28 – July 1, 2028 | George R. Brown Convention Center, Houston, TX | — | future |
| **NACAC 2028** | Oct 5–7, 2028 | Georgia World Congress Center, Atlanta, GA | — | future |
| **HOSA ILC 2029** | June 26–30, 2029 | Orange County Convention Center, Orlando, FL | — | future |

⚠️ **Verify HOSA ILC 2027 before booking.** HOSA's own ILC page lists
**Philadelphia, June 22–25, 2027**, and several state affiliates (NC, NE, SC)
match. But a few state sites (Iowa, Vermont) still list **Baltimore, June
23–26, 2027** — almost certainly a stale earlier schedule. Confirm against
hosa.org directly before booking travel.

### Ranked recommendation for year one

**1. State HOSA leadership conferences (Feb–April) — best ROI by a wide margin.**
Cheap or free to attend, hyper-targeted, and the advisors are right there and
not yet exhausted. Northeast states (PA, NY, NJ, MA, CT) are drivable for you,
which collapses the cost to gas and a hotel night. Start here.

**2. HOSA ILC 2027 — Philadelphia, June 22–25.** Drivable from the Northeast,
which is a genuine cost advantage you should exploit while it lasts (2028 is
Houston, 2029 Orlando). Every HOSA advisor in the country in one hall.
Caveat: ILC is primarily a *student competition* event and advisors are busy
chaperoning, so exhibit-hall conversations are short. Go with handouts and a
QR code, not a demo station.

**3. Regional ACAC conferences (spring).** NACAC has 23 affiliate
organizations running their own annual conferences, mostly February through
April. For you that means NEACAC, NYSACAC, PCACAC. Far cheaper and far more
accessible than the national event, and the attendees are actual high school
counselors rather than college admissions officers. 2026 affiliate dates ran
Feb–March (Indiana Feb 23–26, Kentucky Mar 4–6, Ohio Mar 9–11, Michigan
Mar 16–17, SACAC Mar 22–23); expect a similar 2027 pattern and check the
affiliate page in the fall.

**4. ASCA Annual 2027 — Columbus, July 17–20.** The broadest school counselor
audience in the country. Registration opens February 2027. Worth doing once
the product is solid, but the audience is far less targeted than HOSA —
these counselors handle scheduling, mental health, and crisis response, and
pre-health planning is a sliver of their week.

**5. NACAC National — skip for year one.** Most expensive, most prestigious,
and the wrong room. Over 200 exhibiting organizations, and the attendee mix
skews heavily toward college admissions offices rather than high school
counselors. Kansas City in 2027 is also a flight, not a drive. Revisit in
year two if the counselor channel is working.

### Exhibiting notes

- [ ] Neither ASCA nor NACAC publishes exhibitor pricing publicly — both are
      request-a-quote. **Get quotes early**; budget lines in the workbook are
      estimates, not confirmed figures.
- [ ] Booth cost is never the real cost. Travel, lodging, shipping, booth
      furnishings, and printed materials typically match or exceed it.
- [ ] Year one, prefer **attending** over **exhibiting**. Walk the floor, meet
      advisors, hand out the pathway sheets. A booth is a year-two decision
      once you know the conversation converts.
- [ ] Speaking beats exhibiting. A free session proposal costs nothing and puts
      you in front of a room that chose to be there. ASCA runs a call for
      proposals well ahead of the conference — watch for the 2028 cycle.

## Part 3 — SEO

### The architectural prerequisite

⚠️ **This blocks everything else in this section.** Your app is a Vite SPA —
the server sends an empty shell and JavaScript builds the page. Google renders
JS slowly and inconsistently, and other crawlers (Bing, and the AI answer
engines that are becoming a real referral source) handle it worse.

You cannot run a content SEO strategy on a client-rendered SPA. See the
infrastructure phase in the launch plan for the split-site fix.

### Page types, in order of value

**1. Long-tail program queries** — highest ROI, lowest competition.
- "direct entry BSN programs in Ohio"
- "CRNA prerequisites"
- "how many clinical hours for PA school"
- "colleges with direct admit nursing"

Low volume individually, high intent, weak existing answers, and you'll have
better structured data than whatever currently ranks. There are hundreds of
these — one per pathway × state.

**2. Comparison pages** — your strongest single asset.
- "CRNA vs anesthesiologist" — high intent, and you already have this content
- "MD vs DO", "PA vs NP", "BSN vs direct entry MSN"

**3. Free public tools** — these attract links, which is what actually moves
rankings.
- Public net price comparison
- The award letter comparison tool (Phase 6a) as a free public page
- A pathway readiness quiz

**4. Broad guides** — do these last.
- "how to become a CRNA" — highest volume, hardest to win, dominated by
  established sites

### Cautions

- [ ] ⚠️ **Programmatic pages from a template at scale are exactly what Google's
      helpful-content and site-reputation updates target.** Each page needs
      something genuinely distinct — your data can support that, a template
      alone cannot. Generating 500 near-identical state pages is a real
      penalty risk.
- [ ] **SEO compounds over 6–12 months.** Start publishing during Phase 4, not
      at launch, or you will ship to silence.
- [ ] Every content page needs a clear path into the free tier. Traffic that
      doesn't convert to a signup is just bandwidth.

## Part 4 — Communities

Large, active, and allergic to marketing. Show up as a builder, not a vendor.

- Student Doctor Network (pre-med, pre-PA, pre-dental forums)
- allnurses (pre-nursing, CRNA sections)
- r/premed, r/nursing, r/prephysicianassistant, r/srna
- HOSA advisor Facebook groups
- School counselor Facebook groups and r/schoolcounseling

- [ ] Post the free tool plainly, disclose that you built it, ask for criticism
- [ ] Answer questions in your area of actual knowledge without linking
- [ ] ❌ Never astroturf. These communities detect it, and being caught ends the
      channel permanently.

## Part 5 — Sequencing

| When | Focus |
|---|---|
| During Phase 4 | Start publishing SEO content. Reach out to 10 counselors. |
| During Phase 5–6 | Build free tier + counselor tier. Attend a state HOSA SLC. |
| Spring before launch | Regional ACAC conference. Refine handouts. |
| Launch | Community posts. Counselor referrals. Free tier live. |
| June after launch | HOSA ILC. |
| Year two | Evaluate ASCA and NACAC exhibiting on real CAC data. |

### The number that decides everything

Track **customer acquisition cost against the license price.** If CAC
approaches $129, the model does not work no matter how good the product is.

Free-tier → paid conversion rate is the second number. It determines whether
the free tier is an acquisition engine or just a hosting bill.
